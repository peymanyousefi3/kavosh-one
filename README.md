# Kavosh One

Private source repository for the multi-academy management platform for Kavosh Al and Robotics Academy Inc.

## Status

The owner authorized continuous backend implementation on 3 November 2026: implementation, tests, review and fixes proceed across milestones without another approval request. Visual UI/UX work still requires owner input. This remains an incomplete backend, not a production-ready academy or accounting platform. `backend-work.json` tracks the full approved scope and remaining dependencies; a passing milestone is not the end of the work.

Implemented: PostgreSQL migrations, tenant row policies and composite references, immutable academy-master identity, multi-role permissions with deny precedence, expiring/scoped bot credentials, shared rate limits, hub/pending-person creation and listing, audit history, credential revocation, bounded JSON/pagination, and transactional audit/outbox/idempotency records.

Human identity uses pinned Better Auth: invitation-only email/password signup, email verification, signed-in phone verification, master new-device MFA, optional MFA for others, trusted devices, encrypted single-use recovery codes, password recovery and session management. Unverified accounts cannot use academy APIs. The encrypted auth-message queue has a separate least-privilege delivery worker with SMTP/Twilio adapters; live providers are **not configured or delivery-verified**. Explicitly authorized portal-notification jobs have an isolated worker; general business-event fanout and timed reminders remain unfinished.

Role-template create/read/update/duplicate, scoped role assignments, effective-access inspection, supervisor hierarchy and exact-payload approval APIs are implemented. Masters self-approve; other actors require a permitted upper human reviewer. Expired/revoked bot credentials, changed authority and target versions invalidate pending proposals. Withdraw/reject history, reasons, audit and atomic rollback are preserved. Scoped denials exclude only affected hubs. Templates are academy-wide policy configuration; person-assignment views redact outside-scope segments. Editable geography/hubs, versioned courses, persistent classes/sessions and reviewed cancellation/rescheduling are also implemented; details and limitations are below.

Visual screens, guardian-linked child contact verification, manual identity-recovery review and the complete accounting, learning, storage and BoldSign workflows remain unfinished. Implemented backend portions and their limitations are detailed below and in `backend-work.json`. No mock verification or public activation bypass exists.

The separate `kavosh-academy` repository contains the existing public-facing website and learning lab. Work here must not overwrite or deploy that project.

### Employee and contractor terms (backend)

Weekly/biweekly cycles are anchor-based; monthly/semimonthly settings clamp to the last day of short months without dropping a declared slot when two slots coincide. Custom dates are explicit. Inactive/ended arrangements stop configured dates without deleting earned history. Responses declare that no bank-holiday adjustment or salary proration is performed. ### Scheduled work-cost forecast (backend)

`GET /api/v1/reports/payroll/scheduled-costs` requires explicit civil `from/through` dates and report `timeZone`, with optional hub, worker and output-currency filters. A single database statement supplies currently recorded terms, schedules, dated staffing and permitted enrollment counts. Financial-report rights intersect private work-record rights before source selection; class/staff/roster permissions separately control teaching sources. Finite bot hub ceilings and tenant policies remain effective. This is a scoped planning report, never a complete academy forecast or an actual cash-flow statement.

Known hourly estimates use unrounded scheduled time and the highest rate once per worker/currency across the visible arrangements. Effective terms follow the session start in each arrangement's declared zone; schedule selection follows the report zone. Cancelled sessions are excluded. Enrolled-headcount rates use joining/leaving dates; future attendance is not invented. Monthly salaries on monthly/semimonthly cycles and explicit per-pay-period units are planned on configured pay dates. Semimonthly allocations conserve every cent. Undeclared partial-period handling, unconfigured annual/other salary allocation, class-bound salaries, project payment dates, tuition allocation, hidden/stale sources and overlapping currencies without FX policy are explicitly unpriced—not silently zero. A currency's overall estimate is null whenever it has unpriced components; known subtotals remain separately identified. Salary pay-date and hourly session-date timing is disclosed; actual payout lag/cash flow, deductions, earned liabilities and payment execution are not inferred.

The synchronous report is bounded to 366 civil days, 100 arrangements, 1,000 revisions, 200 staffing/session/derived-hourly sources and 2,000 salary events or issue records. Oversized requests fail explicitly without partial totals; background report execution remains required. Currency filtering changes output only, so a hidden output currency cannot suppress an FX-overlap warning in the selected currency. Pinned currency precision normalizes exactly; no exchange rate is guessed. Worker estimates and dated salary events are chart-ready data, not a visual interface. Full salary policies, project/percentage estimation, all-income/all-expense cash/accrual forecasts, saved presets, exports and scheduled delivery remain required.

Salary components can now declare a reviewed `forecastAllocation`: `calendar_pay_dates` divides the configured monthly/yearly budget among the actual configured payment slots in that complete reference month/year, with stable largest-remainder allocation preserving every cent even in 53-pay-date years. `fixed_divisor` uses the explicitly supplied divisor and half-away rounding per payment; it does **not** claim that the year's payments equal an annual budget, especially in an extra-pay-date year. The report identifies the reference period and allocation policy. Computation is cached once per component/reference period, not repeated for each pay date.

Each planned salary event identifies its divisor, rounding rule and whether the complete reference-period allocation conserves the configured budget; a partial report is not itself promised to sum to that budget. Aggregate reference-calendar walks are limited to 40,000 civil days per synchronous request, with an explicit background-job-required failure beyond that bound.

Proration defaults to `manual`, leaving a partial reference period unpriced. An explicitly reviewed `none` choice permits the declared full per-date planning amounts; it is not an earned-pay or employment-law ruling. Calendar-day/workday earned proration, statutory rules and actual payroll remain required. Existing terms and pending exact payloads are not rewritten or given a policy during upgrade; earlier versions retain their original behavior. Policy changes use the same private upper-human/master/beneficiary/bot approvals as other compensation changes. Native validation independently checks allowed policy keys, divisors and proration choices.

### Automation rule configuration and simulation (backend)

`/api/v1/automations/rules` supports reviewed create/replace, immutable versions, private editor history and disabled independent copies of an explicitly pinned source version. `automations.view/edit` are separate sensitive permissions, scoped to a hub/geography or the school. Ordinary changes require an upper human; masters self-approve and bots propose only. Current permissions, source versions and named-recipient hub/status changes are rechecked. Copying holds shared policy/topology locks while authorizing both source and destination; a later source edit does not break a retry of the same pinned copy. Audit/outbox records redact rule text and conditions. Native exact-payload/contiguous-revision/applied-reviewer constraints, immutable ownership, narrow timestamp privileges and tenant policies protect persistence.

The registered language supports exact/prefix domain-event selectors, bounded `all/any/not`, typed scalar/membership/text comparisons, exact decimal-string comparisons and timestamp comparisons. Missing or incompatible comparison inputs remain unknown, including under `not`; explicit `exists/missing` tests are available. Decimal comparisons never use floating-point money. Conditions have at most 200 nodes, depth 8 and 16,000 compact UTF-8 bytes; native JSONB validation has a 32,000-byte serialization guard plus the same structural limits. User-supplied scripts, SQL, regular expressions and prototype traversal are not permitted. The language catalogue is available at `/automations/language`; it identifies currently registered operators/actions rather than promising arbitrary future adapters.

Notification actions configure portal/email/SMS channels, master/actor-supervisor/named-human audiences, English/French/Simplified-Chinese plain-text templates, mode, recipient/daily/SMS limits, cooldown and a maximum chain-depth of three. Only registered envelope tokens are interpolated; arbitrary attributes cannot be inserted into a message. Critical source severity cannot be downgraded. Titles reject line breaks and templates reject blank content. Named-recipient authorization uses the shared batched person selector, not one database query per recipient. A reviewed disable-only change remains possible after old recipients leave the administrator's visible hub; re-enabling or modifying the definition rechecks the audience.

`POST /automations/rules/{id}/preview` is read-only, including in a read-only school. It evaluates an explicitly unverified caller sample and can render a selected language with English fallback. It never fetches private source attributes, resolves recipient eligibility, consumes budgets, queues work, sends messages or calls an external service. `enabled` is configuration intent, **not** an active runtime: configuration views explicitly disclose `autonomousEventFanoutEnabled: false` and worker liveness as not observed; preview-operation side effects remain false. Explicit durable portal dispatch, deduplication, limits and inbox/read state are now implemented as described below; cross-domain automatic event fanout, remaining alert adapters, scheduled/time-based triggers, report actions and real authorized provider delivery remain unfinished. No visual rule designer or other UI is implemented here.

### Server-bound automation events (backend)

### Durable portal notifications (backend)

`POST /api/v1/automations/rules/{id}/dispatch` processes one real, currently authorized approval event against the current approved rule version. Separate scoped `automations.execute` rights are required; ordinary view/edit rights do not imply execution. An approved `automatic` configuration can produce actual persistent portal notifications. Human and finite-scope bot callers use the same API. This endpoint does not start a daemon or make an external send; it accepts no arbitrary source attributes or browser actions. The isolated queued worker reuses this same domain engine.

Current active human recipients must have linked login accounts and their **own current underlying case permissions**, including payroll/financial/file/person privacy bindings. Producer and bot grants are never lent to them. Master, current supervisor-chain and named-human audiences are supported. Shared grant expansion and one batched case predicate avoid queries per recipient. A recipient does not need rule-editor access to read their own message, but verified login and current source access are checked again on every inbox read.

`GET /notifications`, `/notifications/{id}` and `POST /notifications/{id}/read` provide an owner-only, newest-first inbox, unread filtering and immutable read receipts. Even the school master cannot impersonate another person's inbox. Source-access revocation hides the notification before pagination and prevents marking it read. Reading a message never approves or resolves its source case. `GET /automations/rules/{id}/runs` provides immutable, permission-filtered execution decisions without recipient identities or message text. Both lists preserve native-microsecond cursors.

Processing is atomic with its messages, retry receipt, redacted audit and outbox publication. One transaction-level rule lock prevents duplicate processing and concurrent budget overruns. A source is processed at most once per rule, even across different retry keys; rule revisions do not implicitly replay old notifications. Per-event recipient limits and UTC-day portal-message budgets reject the whole delivery set when exceeded. Cooldowns suppress repeat recipient messages. Budget timestamps use the same server transaction clock as message timestamps. Native guards prevent content/history rewriting, source mismatches, critical-severity downgrades, incomplete run/message counts and post-commit message appends; application roles cannot supply server timestamps or transaction identities.

This is **explicit API-driven portal processing**, not a completed autonomous notification service. `dry_run`, `approval_required` and unmatched rules retain a decision without messages; the review-required outcome does not yet create a dispatch approval case. Email/SMS selections are explicitly unavailable, never silently reported as sent or queued. Immediate background jobs and bounded retries are implemented below. Timed reminders, cross-domain sources, remaining alert adapters, reviewed execution/replay, per-channel queues/retries, provider delivery, preferences and report delivery remain required. No outbound request or visual interface is implemented here.

### Isolated immediate-notification worker (backend)

`POST /api/v1/automations/rules/{id}/queue` persists an **immediate requested job**, not a delivery or a dispatch-approval case. It stores a rule/event/version/locale and an audited reference to the authenticated human session or bot credential—never a bearer, password, cookie, raw session token or message payload. Ordinary current execute/rule/source permissions apply before enqueueing. There are at most 200 outstanding jobs per requesting person. Queue references, original requester and original creation/expiry timing are immutable; jobs expire after one day without deleting their source/history.

`GET /automations/rules/{id}/jobs`, `GET /automations/jobs/{id}` and owner/authorized-upper `POST /automations/jobs/{id}/cancel` expose permission-filtered state and fixed error codes, not private credential/lease/user identifiers. Explicit resubmission after a failed/cancelled job creates a new linked generation with the current verified identity; old history is never rewritten. Pending or succeeded duplicates return the prior job. A new rule version must be explicitly selected, and old queued definitions cannot silently turn into new ones. A source remains deliverable at most once per rule through the shared engine, including across different initiators and job generations.

`pnpm notifications:process` is a separate, default-off process configured only with its own non-owner `kavosh_automation_worker` database login and an explicit academy allowlist. It rejects shared web/operator/identity/file/provider credentials. This role inherits the domain runtime permissions for tenant-scoped processing, but is rejected for web requests; neither database role can inherit the private queue broker or directly read identity/private credential tables; the worker environment excludes provider credentials. Schema-creation privileges are also rejected. Only the isolated broker functions can claim jobs globally, look up a queued credential reference or update leases/retries. That broker's cross-school policy applies only to queue references, not private domain payloads.

Workers lease one job using indexed `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`. They reauthenticate the original queued principal against current stored identity/credential state, including email, phone, session expiry/MFA, bot revocation and finite capability/hub ceilings. Current role/source/recipient permissions are evaluated again by the same dispatch engine. The worker does **not** substitute the approver, master or its own database role as the requesting user. Rule changes, revoked access and unavailable schools cancel the attempt rather than broadening access. This immediate-job mechanism intentionally does not outlive the initiating login/credential authorization; future persistent scheduled automation needs its own reviewed non-interactive authorization.

Job success, messages, run, retry receipt, audit and outbox commit in **one database transaction**. A final exclusive lease fence prevents cancellation or replacement leases from allowing prepared messages to commit. Expired leases can be reclaimed; old tokens cannot authorize/finalize work. Transient failure rolls back domain changes before a separate retry update, with exponential backoff and five attempts maximum. Cleanup is bounded to 100 expired/exhausted jobs per poll. This provides idempotent local persistence, not an exactly-once guarantee for future external delivery. API liveness fields say `not_observed`; they do not pretend to monitor a deployed worker. Run provenance distinguishes immediate API requests from actual queued jobs.

The actual daemon, compiled JavaScript entrypoint and self-contained production package have been tested locally with a generated restricted login, a real stored job and clean signal shutdown. `pnpm notifications:build` emits JavaScript; `Dockerfile.notification-worker` packages only the locked `pg`/`zod` runtime and compiled modules, without the website or TypeScript runner. Local Docker is unavailable: actual image build, default-off diagnostic, non-root UID, read-only owned files, zero effective capabilities, no-new-privileges and minimal-dependency checks are registered in GitHub CI, not claimed as already verified. The worker has not been deployed to the VPS. Automatic custom-rule fanout, remaining domain/security alert adapters, scheduled/time-based triggers, reviewed dispatch/replay, persistent automation credentials, provider queues/delivery, preferences and report delivery remain unfinished. No external messages, payments, UI or deployment changes are included.

## Backend development

Schema 53 derives immutable source-permission bindings for approval history, including older records. Its offline migration operator must have PostgreSQL superuser or RLS-bypass authority so the backfill covers every school; the migration refuses to run without it. Never give those privileges or credentials to application or worker accounts. ## Human identity setup and flow

1. Apply migrations using the operator environment. Provision three **different** non-owner database logins: `kavosh_runtime` member for business APIs, `kavosh_identity` member for authentication, and `kavosh_delivery` member for the delivery worker. Never grant them `kavosh_identity_policy` or ownership/elevated roles. Identity is stored in its own private schema, not a separate physical database.
2. Configure web-only `APP_ORIGIN`, `AUTH_SECRET` (random, at least 32 characters), `IDENTITY_DATABASE_URL`, `DATABASE_URL` and, in production, `EDGE_AUTH_SECRET`. Keep migration credentials out of both web and worker environments. Production database TLS must verify certificates. Production origin must be HTTPS and exactly an origin, without trailing slash or path.
3. Provision the academy, then run `pnpm auth:invite` in the operator environment with `INVITE_ACADEMY_ID`, `INVITE_PERSON_ID`, `AUTH_SECRET` and `MIGRATION_DATABASE_URL`. The pending person's saved email is the invitation destination. Issuance expires old invitations and prints a private, single-use 48-hour invitation; do not put that output in shared logs. Invitation issuance is operator-only in this milestone.
4. The future interface supplies that invitation via `X-Kavosh-Invitation` to `POST /api/auth/sign-up/email`. The person verifies their email link, signs in, verifies a phone they control, then signs in again. Phone verification revokes existing sessions rather than upgrading an old session. A master completes SMS MFA, then accepts the invitation using `POST /api/auth/accept-invitation`. Only successful verified master acceptance activates a provisioning academy. Accepting an ordinary invitation grants no role automatically.
5. Use `/api/auth/memberships` to select an academy, then send `X-Academy-Id` with the host-only session cookie to business APIs. Membership, verification, MFA, academy state and roles are checked server-side. Cookies never authorize access to a foreign academy. Cookie-authenticated mutations require the exact configured Origin.

Every new master device requires an extra step; explicit trust lasts at most 30 days and rotates on use. Others can enable SMS or authenticator MFA. Recovery-code generation requires a fresh verified session and password; store the returned codes securely offline. Password reset/change revokes sessions and remembered-device grants without disabling MFA. There are no security questions or phone-only login/reset endpoints. Changing a verified phone and lost-all-factor recovery require a separately designed, audited support process; no unsafe bypass is provided.

The login interface should guide the user one task at a time (create account → verify email → verify phone → enter academy), automatically choosing the necessary next step, with plain-language errors and resend cooldowns. It must never read/store session tokens, put invitation/reset secrets in analytics, or reveal account existence in recovery messages. Reset links currently target the reserved `/account/recover#token=…` route; that interface is not built, so do not send onboarding messages to real users yet.

`pnpm auth:deliver` runs separately with `DELIVERY_DATABASE_URL`, the same `AUTH_SECRET`, SMTP settings and Twilio credentials from `.env.example`. It leases encrypted messages, retries failures up to three times with backoff, stops expired messages and scrubs payloads after delivery/expiry/final failure. SMTP requires TLS and valid certificates; Twilio calls use a fixed HTTPS endpoint. Provider acceptance is not proof of inbox/handset delivery. A crash after provider acceptance can cause a duplicate retry (at-least-once delivery). Real sender/domain/number setup, delivery receipts, regional regulatory registration and provider billing remain deployment gates.

At the reverse proxy, overwrite `X-Kavosh-Client-IP` from the trusted connection, remove client-supplied proxy headers and inject `X-Kavosh-Edge-Key`. Reject direct access to the application port. The application fails closed on missing/wrong production edge credentials. Add edge request timeouts, body limits, connection/rate limits and upstream DDoS protection; the app limiter alone is not DDoS protection. Never log query strings on verification endpoints, credentials, request bodies or cookies.

Retain `AUTH_SECRET` securely for encrypted recovery codes/messages and invitation digests; unplanned rotation makes them unusable. The running delivery worker hourly purges expired sessions/challenges, budgets older than two days, and expired invitations/scrubbed delivery rows after seven days. This does not delete academy records or audit/security history. Recovery-code regeneration signs out existing sessions; the interface must explain saving codes before signing in again. Before any production launch recheck Better Auth's announced 3 November security release and apply its stable fixes when published; 1.7.15 was the latest stable during this implementation check, not a guarantee against unpublished issues.

## API contract

`public/openapi.json` describes the implemented endpoints. `pnpm api:generate` updates the contract from the actual authentication and business schemas. Business endpoints accept either an expiring bot bearer token (academy derived from token) or a verified human cookie plus an authorized academy selection. Mutations require a UUID `Idempotency-Key`. People creation makes a pending record, not a login or enrollment.

The academy catalogue supports editable countries, provinces/regions, cities, hub contact/address/map coordinates, explicit time zones/currencies, multilingual labels and versioned courses with ordered modules. Hub-scoped readers see only permitted geography; shared and hub-specific courses have separate editing checks. Updates require the current version and preserve omitted fields. Sending a modules array replaces that ordered outline and retains the prior immutable snapshot. Archive rather than delete historical records. Active children prevent geographic archival. Minimal legacy hubs are flagged `setup_complete=false` until their location/time-zone/currency settings are explicitly supplied; no values are guessed. Course bodies are limited to 1 MiB and 100 modules; ordinary business bodies remain limited to 16 KiB. Classes, calendars, enrollment and the other items marked incomplete in `backend-work.json` are not implemented by this catalogue batch.

`POST /api/v1/calendar/preview` compiles weekly patterns, interval weeks, explicit irregular dates, overnight sessions and named excluded dates in the selected hub's explicitly configured time zone. It needs hub viewing rights, has a 128 KiB body limit, does not require an idempotency key, and is allowed during read-only service because it saves no domain records. Each weekly request covers at most 366 inclusive dates, with at most 500 total sessions. Ambiguous/nonexistent local times are rejected unless the caller explicitly selects earlier/later resolution; results retain requested and resolved local times, UTC instants, offsets, time-zone database version and actual duration. Overlapping sessions within the same compiled class schedule are rejected, while touching intervals are allowed. An unmatched exclusion remains visible rather than disappearing. The maintained Temporal polyfill supplies date/time-zone calculations rather than a custom DST algorithm.

`/api/v1/classes` persists classes, generated or configured codes, a pinned curriculum revision and an atomic first schedule. Append further schedule batches with the current class version; existing sessions are never silently replaced. A class retains its original time zone when hub settings change. Class mode/link changes set defaults for later sessions only. Session/schedule views are permission-filtered, paginated, and preserve holiday evidence. The PostgreSQL migration account must be permitted to install the standard `btree_gist` extension: its exclusion constraint prevents same-class session overlaps even during concurrent writes. Touching intervals and different classes at the same time remain valid.

`POST /api/v1/sessions/{id}/change` requests cancellation, restoration, rescheduling or online/in-person delivery changes with a reason. `sessions.request` grants request authority; `classes.edit` plus upper-human approval authority is required to review. Only the human master self-approves. Pending requests do not alter the calendar. Changed session data, revoked rights and time conflicts invalidate approval. Changes retain session identity and audit/outbox evidence; neither restoring a cancelled slot nor appending a schedule may create an overlap. Active classes prevent hub archival; scheduled future sessions prevent class archival. Dated administrative staff/student assignments are implemented below. Signed admission, payroll/report eligibility, calendar pathways and actual parent notification delivery remain incomplete.

Role assignments accept academy-wide `allHubs=true` or selected `hubIds`, `classIds` and `personIds`. The capability catalogue reports supported `scope_kinds`; a class selector cannot accidentally activate unrelated academy-wide feature rights. A selected class grants neither the whole hub nor access to unrelated people. A selected person filters basic people list/detail responses; it does not automatically reveal that person's other classes or restricted care/payroll data. Explicit class/person denials subtract from broad feature rights and cannot be undone by another allow role. Replacement assignments are checked as a complete allow/deny bundle against the delegator's authority. Reviewers must cover the exact typed case scope, not merely its parent hub name. Bot hub ceilings still constrain all record grants. Selectors use tenant-composite foreign keys and are reloaded with each request, with hidden assignment segments redacted from limited viewers. Each assignment supports up to 1,000 IDs of each kind, with 2,000 total selected resources and 128 KiB per access-replacement request.

Country, province/region and city permissions use `countryIds`, `regionIds` and `cityIds`. They include future hubs within the selected hierarchy, including initially empty areas. Finite hub authority cannot be delegated as dynamic geographic authority. Geographic and fixed-record denials survive delegation; incomplete legacy addresses fail closed when a regional denial might apply. Bot credential ceilings remain finite. Topology locks protect permission expansion and writers refresh their rights after waiting for a hub move. Academy-wide policy features cannot be activated by selecting a city.
